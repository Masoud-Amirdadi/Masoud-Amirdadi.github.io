Emergency Response

(Doctoral Research)

Fire Emergency Response

How can fire departments improve emergency response when multiple incidents compete for limited resources?

This study combines queueing theory, optimization, and GIS to support fire-service planning. It considers incidents requiring multiple engines and competing calls to evaluate resource availability, service coverage, and response performance.

Key Features

  • Queueing-based resource availability and delay estimation
  • Multi-engine emergency response modelling
  • Robust location-allocation optimization
  • GIS-based service area planning and visualization
  • Response time evaluation and performance improvement

Vision Zero, Post-Crash Care

How can emergency response planning improve post-crash care and reduce delays following road collisions?

This study develops a decision-support framework combining optimization, GIS, transportation networks, and ML to improve emergency resource allocation and accessibility after road collisions, while maintaining response performance for other emergencies.

Key Features

  • Emergency Response and post-crash care assessment
  • GIS-based road network and accessibility analysis
  • Multi-objective resource allocation optimization
  • ML-based crash risk estimation
  • Response resilience and scenario evaluation

Traffic Digital Twins

(Internship Project)

How can real-world traffic data be used to simulate and improve traffic operations?

This ongoing project uses stationary LiDAR data to develop traffic simulations and digital twins of urban corridors, integrating traffic flow modelling and RL to evaluate and improve traffic operations.

Key Features

  • Vehicle trajectory extraction from stationary LiDAR data
  • Traffic corridor simulation and model calibration
  • Development of data-driven traffic digital twins
  • RL-based traffic signal optimization
  • Evaluation of traffic flow, congestion, and operational improvements

Ongoing Project

This project is led by Mehdi Nourinejad, Associate Professor of Civil Engineering at York University and an Amazon Scholar at Amazon Science.

Reverse Logistics

(Master’s Thesis)

Robust Fuzzy Network Design

How can companies encourage product returns while managing uncertainty in collection rates?

This study connects condition-based buyback offers with collection-network design. Robust fuzzy optimization accounts for differences between expected and actual product returns, supporting decisions under uncertainty.

Key Features

  • Buyback incentives based on used-product condition
  • Uncertain product return rates
  • Robust fuzzy optimization
  • Network design and collection planning
  • Lagrangian relaxation for efficient solution

Credibility-Based E-Waste Collection

How can used electronic products be collected efficiently for recovery and recycling?

This study integrates buyback incentives and collection-network planning to support e-waste recovery. It models uncertain consumer return behaviour and examines how confidence requirements affect collection decisions and costs.

Key Features

  • Collection center location and allocation
  • Product-condition-based buyback offers
  • Fuzzy modelling of product return probabilities
  • Optimization of collection quantities and product flows
  • Credibility-based uncertainty management

Healthcare OR

(Collaborative Research)

How can blood supply networks respond effectively to sudden demand increases and disruptions?

This study develops an optimization framework to strengthen blood supply networks during demand surges and disruptions. It combines coordination between facilities, backup supply, inventory buffers, and donation incentives to improve blood availability while managing costs.

Key Features

  • Interconnected blood supply networks
  • Donation incentive strategies
  • Reducing shortages and operational costs
  • Multi-objective optimization
